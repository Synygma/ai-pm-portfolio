# AI-niche playbook

## Commentator vs practitioner versions

| Type | Commentator (avoid) | Practitioner (write this) |
|---|---|---|
| Tool breakdown | "Top 10 AI tools for PMs" | "I used [tool] for 3 months on real PRDs. 3 things it does better than [alt], 1 dealbreaker." |
| Project build | "Built an AI app over the weekend 🚀" | Scope, hours, cost, what broke, screenshot, what you'd do differently |
| Hot take | "Prompt engineering is dead" | A take tied to a describable observation |
| News commentary | Summarizing the press release | Hands-on verdict within 48h: upside, downside, who should switch |
| Research translation | "New study says AI…" | One finding, what it changes in practice, link in comments |
| Career lesson | "7 steps to become an AI PM" + 8 links | "What I got wrong in my first AI PM interview loop" with the actual question |
| Enterprise adoption | "Enterprises must embrace AI" | Activation vs retention numbers, the blocker, the change that unblocked it |

## Five anti-hype rules
1. No claim without a mechanism or a measurement.
2. Name the model, version and date.
3. Include the failure.
4. Translate it into a PM decision: what should a team do differently on Monday?
5. Stay in 3 lanes (e.g. evals · enterprise rollout · AI PM careers) to build the profile–topic match the 2026 ranker uses.

## What the 8 verified AI posts showed (n=8, anecdotal)
- The top 5 by comments had no question CTA, no link in the body and no hashtags, and all had a first-hand element (a test, a class, an office visit, early access, a family story).
- Comments per 100 reactions: release verdict 30.4; prediction with field notes 16.5; scene story 15.4; test result 13.9; paradox 13.8; teaching field report 9.3; news + 6-link list 10.9; roadmap with 8 links + stacked hard CTA 6.1.
- Lesson: evidence beats commentary. Links and hard CTAs in the body correlate with weaker conversation.
