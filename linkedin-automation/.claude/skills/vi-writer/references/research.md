# Researching the industry for a post

The post's credibility comes from specific, recent, verifiable evidence plus the author's clear interpretation. Commentary without evidence is what the 2026 feed demotes ("recycled", "low-substance") and what readers scroll past.

## Source tiers (prefer higher)
1. **Primary:** research papers and benchmarks (arXiv, conference papers, Stanford AI Index), regulators and standards bodies (SEC, FINRA, EU AI Act texts, NIST, central banks, FCA), company filings, earnings calls, official engineering/product blogs and model cards.
2. **Large credible surveys:** Gartner, McKinsey, BCG, Deloitte, PwC, Bain, MIT/NANDA, a16z, Menlo, IBM, Microsoft Work Trend Index. State the sample size if available.
3. **Reputable reporting:** Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, The Information, The Verge, TechCrunch, Ars Technica.
4. **Practitioner writing:** well-known builders' blogs and newsletters (cite by name). Good for frameworks and examples, weak for statistics.
Avoid: SEO listicles, vendor "stat roundup" pages, content farms, numbers you can't trace back to an origin.

## Search plan (batch 3–6 searches in one turn)
- `<topic> 2026 study OR survey`: the headline number
- `<topic> benchmark accuracy` or `<topic> evaluation results`: hard evidence
- `<topic> company rollout results` / `<topic> failure OR incident OR lawsuit OR fine`: a concrete real-world example
- `<topic> regulator guidance` (for regulated domains such as finance, health, legal)
- `<topic> success OR ROI` to find the counter-evidence and caveat
Use mode "standard"; use "extended" only if results are thin or stale.

## Verification rules
- Open the source for any number that will appear in the hook or the takeaway's support.
- Record exact wording, the number, the date published, and the sample/method if stated.
- Prefer the original over a write-up that cites it. If you only have a secondary source, attribute it that way ("as reported by Reuters").
- Dates: say "in 2026" or "this year" only if true relative to today's date.
- If sources conflict, use the primary one or give the range, and mention the conflict in posting notes.
- Don't overstate: a survey of intentions is not adoption, a benchmark is not production, a pilot is not a rollout.

## Evidence pack format (keep it in your working notes, show a summary in "Evidence used")
| # | Claim / number | Source | Date | URL | Tier |
Aim for: 1 headline number · 1 named real-world example · 1 counter-point/caveat · 1 recent change · 0–2 extras.

## Writing with borrowed evidence, in the author's voice
- Attribute in plain words in the body: "Per Gartner's 2026 CFO survey…", "In [Bank]'s Q2 call…", "A Stanford benchmark found…".
- The author adds: the pattern across sources, what it means for product teams, the decision to make. That interpretation is what makes it thought leadership rather than a news summary.
- Allowed first-person: opinions and readings ("My read:", "What I take from this:", "I keep coming back to…"). Not allowed: invented actions or experiences.
