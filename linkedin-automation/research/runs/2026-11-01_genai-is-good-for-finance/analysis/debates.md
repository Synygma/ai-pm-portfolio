# Debates, conflicts and pushback

## Stance check
User stance: "genai is good for finance". **Verdict: supports, with nuance.** UBS (608 live use cases, 12,700+ agents) and the BoE/FCA survey (86% of firms using AI, 56% of systems GenAI) show real adoption at scale. But the evidence is adoption counts, not financial outcomes, and only 2% of UK firms give agents broad autonomy. The post argues "good for finance where it assists people; proof of value is the open question".

## Relation to the earlier run
The 2026-11-01 run "genai-finance-is-bad" showed weak benchmark scores on financial modeling (34.5%). Together the two posts say the same thing from two sides: strong as an assistant, weak as an autonomous analyst. This post uses none of that run's sources.

## Window note
No substantive trend fell inside the strict 72h window. trend-captain widened it to 7 days. UBS's release (28 Oct) is about 4 days old, with coverage continuing to 31 Oct. The post says "this week" only implicitly ("Q3 2026 results"), and the BoE date is stated explicitly (26 October).

## Conflicting figures and how they were resolved
- UBS says +81% YoY on 608 use cases. The Q3 2025 call figure of 340 implies +79%. Used UBS's own "+81%" and didn't compute from 340.
- BoE: 86% vs 75% in 2024. The page warns the samples differ, so the comparison isn't used.

## Strongest counter-arguments
1. "12,700 agents is sprawl, not success." The release doesn't say how they're governed or audited (AI Weekly). The post's PM advice (an owner per agent, scoped access) answers this directly.
2. "Counts aren't value." Agreed, and the post says so. PYMNTS: only 5–10% of large enterprises report full payback.
3. "Personal agents are glorified chat assistants." Possibly. UBS doesn't define the term. The post calls them "small assistants" and doesn't overclaim.

## Likely pushback and suggested replies
- "Agent count is a vanity metric." → "Agreed, which is the point of the last section. What metric would you put next to it?"
- "UK survey ≠ global banks." → "True. It's 196 UK firms. But the 2% broad-autonomy number matches what US bank CEOs described on Q3 calls: assistive first."
- "Banks are blocking third-party agents from customer accounts." → "Different problem: that's external agents acting for customers. This post is about internal assistants."
