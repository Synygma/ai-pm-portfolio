UBS employees now run more than 12,700 AI personal agents.
Not a pilot. That's roughly one for every nine people at the bank.

UBS's Q3 2026 results this week also list 608 live AI use cases in production, up 81% in a year.

It isn't just UBS. A Bank of England and FCA survey (26 October) found 86% of UK financial firms use AI, and GenAI is 56% of the systems they reported.

So yes, GenAI is good for finance. But look at where it works.
80% of UK firms have AI agents deployed. Only 2% give them broad autonomy.

The value comes from thousands of small assistants that help people search, prepare and draft while a human decides. Not from an AI analyst running the books.

The gap is proof. UBS reports counts, not dollars.

If you're building for a bank, ship the unglamorous platform: an owner per agent, scoped data access, a human-executes default, and one outcome metric per agent set before launch.

For those in banking and finance: what metric would you use to prove an internal AI agent paid off?
